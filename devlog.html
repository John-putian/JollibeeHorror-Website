Art Pipeline

Our art direction starts with a place that feels familiar. The restaurant at dusk uses warm light and clean shapes so the children feel safe walking past it. When the lights go out, the same red and gold storefront becomes the backdrop for a single abandoned balloon. Keeping the location recognizable makes that transition more effective than introducing a different building for every shot.

The visual work divides into backgrounds, props, characters, and interface elements. Full-screen story stills use a fixed 16:9 layout and leave dialogue to Unity. Interactive props use individual transparent PNGs. Open and closed versions share their canvas dimensions so a cabinet can change appearance without jumping on the screen. For character animation, each animation strip uses a consistent cell size and foot baseline. This makes it easier to slice clips and identify when a pose shifts unintentionally.

The user interface uses rusty metal borders with flat centers. We can stretch these panels around different text lengths with Unity’s 9-slice setting. The confession letter uses a separate paper background. Its text remains editable, so the team can correct the wording or adjust readability without repainting a sprite.

The final integration pass should compare the new artwork with the current playable scenes. Check that feet meet the floor, that drawers and doors keep their position when states change, and that pickups remain visible in darkness. A full-resolution image can still be hard to read at game scale. Inspect important interactables and the objective display in both a window and fullscreen, then record which assets were accepted or changed. Keep the final changes in the release notes.

Boss Design

Each boss encounter teaches the player to respond to one readable signal before introducing a harder variation. In the dining room, Jollibee is the first test of whether the player can use the environment under pressure. The live cable matters because it turns prior knowledge about the restored power into an actual decision. Its visual and audio states should help players recognize when they can lead the boss toward it.

Popo’s kitchen uses two oil fryers. The warning before the oil burst should be distinct from the burst itself, so observing the room gives the player time to react. Popo reaches for a fry in his cap before throwing it. Matching the projectile spawn to the release frame helps players connect the animation to the hazard. During Meteor Fries, the casting pose and falling zone need to remain readable even when several projectiles share the screen.

Hetty changes the kind of attention the player needs. In the freezer, danger comes from both the ceiling and floor-level vents. Icicles need a visible warning before they drop. A spinning whip signals when shots will be deflected in her later fight. Against final Jollibee, jumping avoids the ground wave, while a timed shield reduces charge damage. The fight ends after ten successful bullet hits. The player should be able to tell a successful hit by the feedback.

During integration, check each cue with the music off, then check the full mix. Record whether players can identify the response after seeing the cue. Adjust timing based on what they missed and how quickly they understood the second attempt.

Playtesting

The release playtest starts from a fresh save in the packaged Windows build. Have one tester play from the main menu without coaching. Ask them to read the tutorial and describe their current objective in their own words. Write down where they pause, miss a prompt, or leave a room without an essential item. Keep the elapsed time and the build version beside each observation.

Use a second pass to test progression and recovery. In the generator lobby, confirm the flashlight, battery, switch, and key cannot leave the player stuck after a restart. Collect the fuses in different orders, let the gas timer expire, then repeat the escape successfully. Check that a checkpoint reload restores the correct inventory and doors. Reconstruct the family photograph, inspect the reverse markings, and try both an incorrect code and the correct vault code.

For combat, verify the cable triggers the dining-room outcome, fryer warnings precede the kitchen hazards, and freezer hazards reset correctly after retry. Test a shot into Hetty’s active shield and another during recovery. Check successful hits against final Jollibee and confirm that jumping and blocking change the outcomes of his attacks. Pause during a timed sequence and verify how its countdown resumes.

Record actual outcomes once testing takes place. A useful report includes the room, steps to reproduce, expected result, observed result, and whether a later build fixed it. Finish by checking the credits, audio settings, and sunrise ending in the same export used for the demo. Save six gameplay captures from that build and use one uninterrupted run as the source for the final trailer edit.
